One of the most common questions we get from new partners is simple: *"Which warehousing model is right for us?"*

The honest answer is that it depends less on company size and more on how predictable your demand is, and how much operational control you want to keep in-house. Here's how to think about each option.

## Shared warehousing — for steady, moderate volume

You pay for the rack or pallet space you actually use, on a transactional basis. This is the default starting point for most SMEs and any brand with seasonal bursts — no long-term commitment, standardized service levels, and the ability to scale down as easily as up.

## Dedicated warehousing — for scale and specialized handling

Once volume is consistent and high enough to justify it, a dedicated facility (or exclusive area within one) gives you a fixed monthly cost, full control over layout and process, and priority access — particularly important for cold chain or other specialized handling requirements.

## Managed warehousing — for brands with their own facility

If you already own or lease a facility but want operational expertise without building an internal ops team, a managed model hands day-to-day running — staffing, process, technology — to your logistics partner while you keep the real estate.

## On-demand warehousing — for spikes and overflow

Promotional events, seasonal peaks, and dead-stock overflow don't need permanent space — they need *temporary* space, fast. On-demand billing (daily variable rates) exists specifically for this, and it's usually the cheapest way to handle a short-term spike without over-committing to capacity you won't need in month two.

## A simple way to decide

Ask yourself two questions:

1. **How predictable is my monthly volume?** Predictable favors Dedicated or Managed. Unpredictable favors Shared or On-Demand.
2. **Do I want to own the facility?** If yes, Managed. If no, Shared, Dedicated, or On-Demand — depending on volume.

Most brands actually end up running a *mix* — dedicated space for core, always-moving SKUs, with shared or on-demand capacity absorbing seasonal peaks.
